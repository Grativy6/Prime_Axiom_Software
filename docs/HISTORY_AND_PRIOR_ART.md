## 1. Evidence boundary

This document keeps four kinds of statement separate:

- **Sourced fact** is directly supported by a linked primary document, museum record, university archive, or research publication.
- **Established mathematics** is a standard result with an inspectable mathematical or formal-library source.
- **Engineering inference** is a conclusion drawn by comparing representations or artifacts. It is not attributed to a source unless the source itself makes the claim.
- **Bounded search result or research hypothesis** reports what this review found or proposes testing. It is not evidence of historical absence, novelty, priority, or universal performance.

Here, *necessary* always means necessary for a stated implementation goal. It does not mean metaphysically necessary for all computation. Analogue, mechanical, electromechanical, and electronic machines prevent a two-state electrical stack from being treated as the only possible computational floor.

Claims of “first” are avoided unless the category is explicit and material to the argument. Institutions with custody of important artifacts also have a natural interest in priority narratives, so their technical descriptions are generally more useful here than their broad priority labels.

## 2. Result in brief

The conventional story

```text
physical distinction -> bit -> binary integer -> Boolean arithmetic -> instruction -> software
```

is neither the historical sequence nor a chain of physical necessities. Material records, positional notation, mechanical arithmetic, external symbolic control, switching logic, and stored-program machines developed in partly independent lineages and later converged.

The history supports a credible architectural fork **after stable state, switching, identity, memory, registers, and counters, but before commitment to one numeric representation and arithmetic unit**. A binary cell can represent occupancy, control, a decimal digit code, a unary counter, an exponent bit, or part of a sparse record. This shows that ordinary binary magnitude is contingent at the representation layer; it does not show that a prime-native alternative is useful.

The central prime representation is already established mathematics:

```text
F(n)(p) = v_p(n)
```

For positive integers, `F(n)` is a finitely supported prime-to-exponent map. Unique factorization identifies positive integers under multiplication with the free commutative monoid generated by the primes. Lean mathlib implements essentially this representation as [`Nat.factorization`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Data/Nat/Factorization/Defs.html). A sparse prime-coordinate value is therefore a valid implementation target, but not a new number system.

The operation asymmetry is real but conditional. Multiplication, exact division, divisibility, `gcd`, `lcm`, powers, and valuations become coordinate-local. Addition, numeric order, arbitrary ingress, and ordinary output do not. Multiplication also does not become costless: exponent coordinates require counters, sparse support must be merged, and prime identities must be represented.

The closest justified architecture is consequently a **typed, multi-domain sidecar or coprocessor**, not a replacement for bits or all integer arithmetic. Build 000 measured a local logical-work advantage when values were born as factor coordinates and stayed multiplicative, while matched-domain dense lanes lost their modeled NAND-count advantage as catalogue coverage grew. Those are bounded results of this implementation. Whether factor structure can amortize its construction and storage on useful real workloads remains an experimental question, not a historical or mathematical conclusion.

## 3. Historical reconstruction: several lineages, not one stack

### 3.1 Persistent marks and operated counters

**Sourced facts.** The [Whipple Museum](https://www.whipplemuseum.cam.ac.uk/explore-whipple-collections/calculating-devices/brief-history-calculating-devices) describes grouped line marks, tally sticks, knots, and counting sticks as long-lived recording practices. The [Smithsonian](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object-groups/the-abacus-the-numeral-frame-and-counters) describes abaci and counting boards on which arithmetic is performed by moving counters; its [soroban record](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/the-abacus-the-numeral-frame-and-counters/the-japanese-abacus) shows that bead value depends jointly on bead state and column position.

**Engineering inference.** A tally-like record needs persistent distinguishable tokens and a correspondence convention, but it does not require radix, zero, carry, or a pre-existing machine integer. It is not physically privileged: it pays storage and traversal proportional to represented quantity. The abacus is a more important branch point than a simple “unary came first” story suggests. Positional value can live in spatial organization and operating practice rather than a numeral string or machine word.

### 3.2 Positional representation as compression

**Sourced facts.** The University of St Andrews [history of Babylonian numerals](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Babylonian_numerals/) documents a base-60 positional system constructed from two basic signs, following earlier non-positional systems. It also describes ambiguity before a placeholder was adopted: the same mark sequence could represent values at different scales, with context carrying the missing information. The source treats the historical reason for base 60 as unsettled.

**Established mathematics and engineering inference.** Positional notation uses a bounded digit alphabet plus position to encode unbounded magnitude. It buys compression by introducing scale, normalization, and empty-position obligations. A zero placeholder addresses positional ambiguity; zero as a number is a separate development. Radix is contingent, and its prime factors determine which rational denominators have terminating expansions. That last fact is relevant to representation design, but it is not evidence for replacing positional arithmetic with factor coordinates.

### 3.3 Mechanical arithmetic exposed the price of carry

**Sourced facts.** The Whipple Museum's [mechanical calculation survey](https://www.whipplemuseum.cam.ac.uk/explore-whipple-collections/calculating-devices/mechanical-calculation) describes automatic carry as a central mechanical difficulty and explains the stepped-drum lineage of the Leibniz calculator. The [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/babbage/engines/) explains that Babbage's Difference Engine used finite differences so polynomial tables could be generated by repeated addition rather than general multiplication or division. Babbage considered radices 2, 3, 4, 5, 12, 16, and 100, then selected decimal based on a mixed judgment about moving parts and everyday familiarity. His Analytical Engine design separated `Store` and `Mill`, used punched-card control, and included branching and iteration.

**Engineering inference.** Carry is not a universal property of quantity. It is a normalization obligation created by bounded positional digits, and mechanical gearing made its propagation cost visible. Leibniz-style multiplication automated a schedule of digit selection, repeated addition, shifting, and carry; the user's named operation was not the machine's lowest primitive. The Difference Engine is a strong counterexample to the idea that a general arithmetic unit must precede useful computation: a workload-specific recurrence removed harder operations from the mechanism. Babbage's radix study is direct evidence that binary was an option, not a mechanical inevitability.

### 3.4 External symbolic control preceded stored programs

**Sourced facts.** Smithsonian histories of [punch cards](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object-groups/punch-cards) and [Hollerith tabulation](https://www.si.edu/spotlight/tabulating-equipment/from-herman-hollerith-to-ibm) describe Jacquard cards selecting threads row by row, Babbage's proposed adaptation of punched control, and Hollerith's punch/tabulate/sort system used for the 1890 United States census.

**Engineering inference.** Hole/no-hole is a two-state distinction whose immediate meaning can be selection and control rather than numeric magnitude. External media can carry a repeatable program without storing instructions in writable data memory. Hollerith's lineage also demonstrates scalable computation organized around classification, counting, and sorting rather than a general-purpose arithmetic unit. Stored programs later changed flexibility, speed, and the code/data boundary; they did not create programmability from nothing.

### 3.5 Boolean algebra met switching after both already existed

**Sourced facts.** George Boole's 1854 [`An Investigation of the Laws of Thought`](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/206199) developed algebraic symbolic logic independently of electrical computers. Claude Shannon's 1938 primary paper, [`A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits`](https://tubes.mit.edu/6S917/_static/2025/resources/shannon38.pdf), explicitly connected symbolic logic to the analysis and synthesis of relay/contact networks. The Computer History Museum records George Stibitz's small [Model K relay adder](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/birth-of-the-computer/4/85), which added two binary digits and led to a larger relay calculator.

**Engineering inference.** Boolean algebra did not arise because digital electronics physically demanded it. Shannon established an exceptionally useful correspondence between an existing algebra and switching networks. Open/closed or low/high states provide robust digital distinctions and economical circuit composition; they do not determine the numeric semantics of the signals.

### 3.6 Binary and decimal crossed physical media

**Sourced facts.** The [Deutsches Museum](https://www.deutsches-museum.de/en/museumsinsel/ausstellung/computers) describes Zuse's Z3 as a programmable binary relay machine. Iowa State's technical history of the [Atanasoff–Berry Computer](https://jva.cs.iastate.edu/operation.php) describes its special-purpose linear-equation workload, explicit base-two choice, regenerative capacitor memory, and electronic logical action. Harvard's [Mark I record](https://chsi.harvard.edu/harvard-ibm-mark-1-function) describes paper-tape control, mechanical storage counters, and shaft synchronization. The primary [`Report on the ENIAC`](https://ftp.arl.army.mil/~mike/comphist/46eniac-report/) describes an electronic decimal machine whose accumulators combined signed ten-digit storage with arithmetic; pulse circuits carried binary events while decade counters represented decimal digits.

**Engineering inference.** Electronics did not force binary numerals, and electromechanical construction did not force decimal. Binary reduces local digit-state complexity but uses more digits; decimal can reduce conversion and digit count while increasing cell and transition complexity. Component reliability, interconnect, arithmetic patterns, and human interface jointly determine the trade. Mark I's drive shaft also shows that synchronization is a coordination requirement, not inherently an electronic clock.

### 3.7 Architectural boundaries stabilized late

**Sourced facts.** ENIAC accumulators fused storage, arithmetic, transfer, and local control. Von Neumann's 1945 [`First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC`](https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/firstdraftofrepo00vonn) and the 1946 Burks–Goldstine–von Neumann [`Preliminary Discussion`](https://www.ias.edu/library/ecp) formalized influential divisions among arithmetic, control, memory, input, and output. The University of Manchester records that the [Small-Scale Experimental Machine](https://curation.cs.manchester.ac.uk/computer50/www.computer50.org/mark1/new.baby.html) ran a stored program on 21 June 1948, with the same random-access memory holding 32-bit two's-complement numbers and instructions.

**Engineering inference.** The modern `register -> ALU -> memory -> decoder` partition is a productive standardization, not a primitive fact. Treating instructions and numbers as same-memory words is likewise a powerful representation unification, not a logical necessity. Build 000 should earn those boundaries rather than assume them at the simulator floor.

## 4. What was forced, and what was contingent?

The table states bounded engineering requirements, not universal laws.

| Problem | Requirement when the problem applies | Historically contingent realization |
|---|---|---|
| Persistent record | A recoverable physical configuration | notch, knot, counter, bead, glyph, gear, charge, magnetic state |
| Digital operation | Distinguishable state regions and reliable transitions | exactly two states; electrical rather than mechanical media |
| Representation | A stable interpretation mapping configurations to objects | tally, radix magnitude, signed magnitude, two's complement, residue tuple, factor coordinates |
| Large quantity from bounded local symbols | Repetition, extensible storage, positional weighting, or compositional structure | radix 2, 10, or 60; linked records; prime/exponent pairs |
| Positional arithmetic | Carry/borrow normalization or an equivalent redundant-digit rule | ripple carry, lookahead, geared carry, deferred normalization |
| Sequential coordination | A way to order or enable transitions | hand operation, shaft timing, relay pulse, electronic clock, asynchronous/dataflow firing |
| Reuse of intermediate results | Persistence/addressability sufficient for the algorithm | counter, accumulator, register bank, delay line, cards, random-access memory |
| Programmability | A selectable, repeatable control description | plugboard, drum, cards, tape, stored instruction words |
| Target arithmetic | A realizable operation basis sufficient for the workload | centralized ALU; distributed accumulators; recurrence engine; structural coprocessor |
| Reliability | Margins and an error/maintenance strategy appropriate to the medium | jamming, redundancy, parity, preventive maintenance, error-correcting codes |

Two boundaries matter. Persistence is required only when a result must survive the act that produced it. Distinguishable discrete regions apply to digital operation, not analogue computation generally.

### How a machine comes to exhibit `1, 2, 3, 4, ...`

The following is an engineering reconstruction, not one universal historical sequence:

1. Associate one persistent mark, token, or event with one item or occurrence.
2. Make a repeatable successor action: add a token or advance one state.
3. Group several unit states into a higher-valued region or token.
4. Interpret a local digit by position, as in an abacus column or geared wheel.
5. Normalize overflow by carry into another position.
6. Encode digits in a physical medium: gear positions, relay patterns, pulses, ring-counter phases, or bit cells.
7. Build transitions that preserve the selected representation invariant.
8. Optionally place transition descriptions in addressable memory with data.

The earliest coherent experimental fork is after stable distinctions can be retained, addressed, counted, and associated with symbolic identities, but before steps 4 and 5 commit the numeric layer to positional magnitude and carry. In the implemented lineage, that is the representation layer above registers and counters. Prime identity cannot be primitive “below distinction”: even a fixed prime lane needs an identity convention, and an unbounded prime namespace needs an encoding or table. Prime coordinates can be an alternative numeric structure above that shared register/counter substrate. That boundary is an engineering conclusion of Build 000, not a historical finding.

### Historical branch points worth retaining in the experiment

- An abacus embodies place value spatially before a conventional machine word exists.
- Base 60 demonstrates that radix affects exact fractions and operating rules without being a natural law.
- Mechanical carry exposes normalization as mechanism and cost.
- The Difference Engine simplified hardware by changing the recurrence rather than accelerating every operation.
- Punched media separated two-state control from binary numeric magnitude.
- Tabulation scaled selection and aggregation without centering a general ALU.
- Shannon's synthesis connected, rather than co-originated, logic and switching.
- Decimal electronics and binary relay machines show that medium does not uniquely determine radix.
- Accumulators preceded a clean, centralized ALU boundary.
- Programmability preceded writable stored programs.

## 5. Established mathematical baseline

Let `P` be the ordinary primes and let `N^(P)` denote finitely supported maps `P -> N`. For `n > 0`, define `F(n)_p = v_p(n)`. Unique factorization gives

```text
(positive integers, multiplication, 1)
    ~=
(finitely supported exponent maps, coordinate addition, zero map).
```

Positive rationals are the group completion, with finitely supported maps `P -> Z`. The Stacks Project records the core [valuation laws](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00IF), and mathlib provides executable definitions and proofs for factorization, reconstruction, injectivity on nonzero naturals, multiplication, powers, and divisibility.

| Ordinary operation or predicate | Coordinate operation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | empty map | exact |
| `a * b` | add exponents | `a,b != 0` |
| exact `a / b` | subtract exponents | fail if an integer exponent would be negative |
| rational `a / b` | subtract signed exponents | zero remains separate |
| `a^k` | scale every exponent by `k` | `k` is still an ordinary integer |
| `a divides b` | coordinatewise `<=` | nonzero positive integers |
| `gcd(a,b)` | coordinatewise `min` | exact |
| `lcm(a,b)` | coordinatewise `max` | exact |
| perfect `k`th power | every exponent divisible by `k` | exact |
| positive size/order | compare `sum e_p log p` | global weighted reduction or reconstruction |

Zero and sign are not cosmetic. `1` is the empty factor map. Zero is absorbing and is not a finite-support valuation vector; mathlib's convenient convention maps both `0` and `1` to an empty factorization, so a semantically injective machine needs an explicit zero tag. Signed integers need a separate unit/sign field. Rationals fit the coordinate structure more naturally because negative exponents make exact cancellation local, but zero remains exceptional.

Addition has limited structure. Valuations satisfy

```text
v_p(a + b) >= min(v_p(a), v_p(b)),
```

with equality when the two input valuations differ. Thus `gcd(a,b)` can be extracted before forming `a/g + b/g`. Equal valuations require information about the residual units; the output can acquire unrelated new prime factors. This justifies an operation that returns a known common factor plus a residual expression. It does not make complete factorized addition coordinate-local.

There is also a structural obstruction to deriving ordinary addition from anonymous prime composition alone. Any permutation of the prime generators extends to an automorphism of the free commutative monoid. Ordinary addition is not invariant under those automorphisms: swapping `2` and `3` maps `2+2=4` to symbols corresponding to `3+3`, while the image of `4` is `9`, not `6`. Ordinary addition therefore requires more structure: conventional prime values, successor/order, an evaluation map, or an independently specified addition operation. Calling primes by increasing index already imports conventional magnitude order.

## 6. Closest prior art, ranked by proximity

The ranking is by proximity to this build's exact representation and proposed machine behavior, not by historical importance. Adjacent work can be closer mathematically than architecturally, or vice versa.

| Rank | Prior art | Proximity and lesson | Important difference or limit |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exact factor maps and valuation vectors | **Mathematically identical.** Mathlib's finite-support factorization is executable direct prior art. | It does not establish a physical instruction set or a mixed-workload advantage. |
| 2 | Monomial exponent vectors and sparse computer algebra | **Computationally almost identical.** Monomial multiplication adds exponents; divisibility and lcm are coordinatewise. | Independent variables do not satisfy evaluated integer relations such as `2+3=5`. |
| 3 | Product-of-exponentials and multiplication/division circuits | Very close succinct representation; equality and order expose compact-input complexity distinctions. | Bases need not be canonical primes, and adding arbitrary sums leads toward general arithmetic-circuit problems. |
| 4 | Fixed factor bases, smooth numbers, and `S`-units | Closest established bounded-lane model; practical algorithms already store exponent vectors over selected primes. | Smooth domains are selected, not closed under ordinary addition, and arbitrary cofactors escape the basis. |
| 5 | Fermi–Dirac factorization | Direct bit-level decomposition of exponent lanes into addresses `(prime, exponent-bit)`. | It is the same factor vector with binary counters; carry is relocated into each exponent. |
| 6 | Logarithmic number systems (LNS) | Closest hardware precedent for multiplication/division becoming addition/subtraction. | Usually approximate; sign, zero, addition, subtraction, and conversion require correction machinery. |
| 7 | Multidimensional logarithmic and double-base systems | Small exponent-lane hardware and sparse sums of terms such as `2^a 3^b`. | Finite bases, redundancy, approximation or costly normalization; not canonical full factorization. |
| 8 | Residue number systems (RNS) | Strongest control architecture for parallel number-theoretic coordinates. Both addition and multiplication are local. | Coordinates are residues, not valuations; range, sign, order, division, and conversion are global concerns. |
| 9 | Gödel-style prime-power coding | Exact structured data represented by prime positions and exponent payloads. | Good sequence encoding is not evidence of a good general numeric ALU; factors are normally known by construction. |
| 10 | Matula numbers and recursive prime indexing | Direct prior art for recursive prime-index/tree encodings. | It produces an elegant tree code while making the `n`th-prime map and ordered enumeration primitive. |
| 11 | Dataflow machines | Natural execution substrate for tagged factor tokens, independent lanes, and lazy expression graphs. | Scheduling architecture does not remove identity, factorization, order, or normalization costs. |
| 12 | Reversible computation | Supplies the correct invariant discipline for retaining inputs/history and accounting for discarded information. | Orthogonal to prime coordinates; factor operations are not inherently reversible. |

### 6.1 Sparse algebra and expression form

Sparse polynomial systems routinely represent a monomial `x_1^e1 ... x_k^ek` by an exponent vector and optimize merges, packing, heaps, and memory layout; Monagan and Pearce survey these [implementation tradeoffs](https://doi.org/10.3390/math7050441). Replacing `x_i` by prime `p_i` makes one factored positive integer look exactly like one evaluated monomial.

The analogy suggests a controlled extension:

```text
factor word       ~ monomial
sum of factors    ~ sparse polynomial
shared expression ~ arithmetic circuit / straight-line program
```

The analogy breaks at evaluation. Polynomial variables are algebraically independent; ordinary primes are fixed integers with additive relations. A lazy sum/product DAG can preserve provenance and postpone factorization, but exact equality, order, and canonicalization may require evaluation. Kaltofen's work on [straight-line-program polynomials](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/89/Ka89_slpfac.pdf) establishes that circuit form can be exponentially more succinct than expansion in related settings, not that ordinary integer sums become easy to factor.

Etessami, Stewart, and Yannakakis analyze [products of exponentials](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1304.5429). Equality can be substantially easier than numeric inequality; the latter depends on controlling linear forms in logarithms when inputs are succinct. Once arbitrary addition and subtraction gates are admitted, [PosSLP](https://doi.org/10.1137/070697926) is the relevant general warning: “retain the expression and compare later” is not a free universal solution. Restricted [power circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.2570) show the positive counterpart—careful normal forms can make selected operations efficient while others trigger growth.

### 6.2 Bounded bases and sidecar coordinates

Factor-base methods in the [number field sieve](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0091539) already use exponent vectors for smooth values. This succeeds because the algorithm filters or constructs values in a chosen basis. For a finite prime set `B`, a more honest machine value is

```text
sign * product_{p in B} p^e_p * opaque_cofactor,
```

with the exact invariant that the cofactor is coprime to the basis. The cofactor must not be mislabeled a prime. Literature on [`S`-unit equations](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01393743) reinforces that additive relations inside a fixed multiplicative basis are exceptional, not an omitted local rule.

RNS is an essential adversarial control. Garner's primary [residue-number-system paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEC.1959.5219515) makes addition, subtraction, and multiplication carry-free and coordinate-local over a bounded dynamic range, while relative magnitude and division are difficult. This disproves any broad claim that number-theoretic coordinates must privilege multiplication over addition. It also motivates factor/valuation state paired with residue or magnitude sidecars, with every projection and reconstruction charged explicitly.

### 6.3 Hardware precedents for relocated arithmetic

Mitchell's binary-log [multiplication/division method](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEC.1962.5219391), the Swartzlander–Alexopoulos [sign/logarithm system](https://doi.org/10.1109/T-C.1975.224172), and Lewis's [LNS addition architecture](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.61042) are the clearest hardware warning against treating “multiplication becomes addition” as the end of the accounting. LNS must pay for nonlinear addition/subtraction correction, near cancellation, zero/sign representation, and conversion.

Multidimensional logarithmic and [double-base systems](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.805158) bring the analogy closer to fixed prime lanes. DBNS represents values as sparse sums of terms such as `2^a 3^b`, and can benefit selected DSP or exponentiation workloads. It is redundant rather than a canonical full factorization, making normalization and conversion part of the design rather than free adapters.

“Fermi–Dirac” factorization names the elementary expansion of each prime exponent in binary: every positive integer is a unique squarefree product of terms `p^(2^k)`. This gives an explanatory circuit address `(p,k)`. Composition is still binary addition within each prime lane, including carries. It is a useful control for showing that factor coordinates relocate conventional arithmetic into exponent counters.

### 6.4 Structured coding and execution are not arithmetic proofs

Gödel-style coding assigns sequence positions to successive primes and entries to exponents; the [Stanford Encyclopedia supplement](https://seop.illc.uva.nl/entries/goedel-incompleteness/sup1.html) gives a modern explanation and [Gödel's 1931 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01700692) is the primary provenance. It establishes prime powers as exact structured storage when factors are known by construction. It does not show that the resulting magnitude is an efficient general arithmetic value.

Matula's [rooted-tree enumeration](https://s2.smu.edu/~matula/Abstract-68.htm) is direct prior art for recursively mapping prime indices and factorization to trees. It shows that recursive prime identity can be elegant and bijective while still importing `nth_prime` and ordinary prime order. Dataflow and reversible-computing work likewise informs machine organization and evidence accounting, but neither changes the algebraic or conversion costs.

## 7. Where the cost goes

Prime coordinates can simplify an operation only after their state, identity, and crossing costs are included.

| Cost boundary | What must be counted |
|---|---|
| Representation | payload bits, support encoding, exponent widths, sign/zero tags, allocator/index metadata, prime tables, caches |
| Prime identity | explicit prime value, increasing index plus `nth_prime` lookup, fixed lane, or intern table plus certification/provenance |
| Composition | sparse support merge, exponent addition and carry, routing, allocation, normalization; not merely “vector addition” |
| Ingress | complete factorization for arbitrary magnitudes; bounded-basis stripping and exact cofactor otherwise |
| Addition/subtraction | common-factor extraction, residual evaluation, newly discovered factors, or expression-DAG growth |
| Equality/divisibility | canonical map equality and coordinate comparison; cheap only when factorization is complete and certified |
| Numeric order | certified logarithmic intervals or exact reconstruction; not lexicographic coordinate order |
| Reconstruction/output | at least the expanded output bit count, plus multiplication/exponentiation work |
| Locality/parallelism | map layout, merge frontier, dynamic lane allocation, cache behavior, routing; independent lanes alone do not settle performance |
| Reversibility | retained operands, failure witnesses, quotients/remainders, history/ancilla, and cleanup |

Any injective representation of `N` distinct bounded values needs at least `ceil(log2 N)` fixed-width bits. Prime coordinates can compress special families such as prime powers, but cannot uniformly compress all values without expansion elsewhere or an external dictionary. A dense lane for every possible prime is untenable at general scale; sparse support or a bounded basis is required.

Prime index is not free. Storing the prime value supports direct reconstruction but costs about `log p` bits for a large factor. Storing its increasing index saves only about `log log p` bits asymptotically and imports prime ordering plus table or `nth_prime` cost. A fixed small-prime bank is hardware-friendly but not closed over arbitrary integers. A dynamic atom ID needs persistent canonical identity and a primality certificate or provenance. An unresolved composite must remain an explicit opaque cofactor.

Ingress from arbitrary binary magnitude to a complete canonical vector is integer factorization. No classical polynomial-time algorithm is known, and no classical superpolynomial lower bound is proved; it would be inaccurate to call hardness a theorem. Lenstra's [elliptic-curve method](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971363) and the number field sieve are classical reference points. Shor's [quantum factoring algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539795293172) changes the computational substrate and does not erase classical conversion cost.

Reconstruction must emit every ordinary output bit. If a compact factor value denotes an `L`-bit integer, materializing that magnitude costs at least `Omega(L)` simply to produce the output. Decimal printing, ordinary addressing, some comparisons, and conventional I/O may force this crossing. A local advantage is therefore most plausible when values are born structurally, remain structural across many operations, and cross domains rarely.

## 8. Research boundary and most defensible architecture

The literature removes several possible novelty claims before implementation begins:

- Full prime-exponent coordinates are established mathematics and executable formal-library prior art.
- Exponent-vector multiplication, divisibility, and lcm behavior are standard sparse-algebra operations.
- Multiplication-as-addition with difficult addition/conversion is established hardware territory in LNS and related systems.
- Finite prime bases, smooth-value exponent vectors, prime-power encodings, recursive prime indexing, and number-theoretic coordinate machines all have substantial prior art.

Build 000 established one bounded local region: across 64 seeded factor-resident pairs over the first eight primes, dense coordinate composition used 981 modeled NAND evaluations (including overflow-status reduction) in every case versus a 59,168 median for equivalently wide transparent binary multiplication, while the two coordinate operands used 128 payload bits versus an 86-bit binary median. It also showed the opposite pressure in the matched-domain sweep: dense composition used fewer modeled NAND evaluations only through the tested bound 128, then lost from 256 through 4,096 as catalogue lanes, payload, and exponent adders accumulated. These results support a specialized sidecar and reject a universal dense store within the declared model; they do not establish a technology-independent crossover or useful application workload.

The remaining open question is empirical and architectural: can a typed value preserve verified factor structure long enough to amortize construction, validation, storage, invalidation, and crossing costs on a named workload? The most defensible Build 001 candidate is a bounded valuation bank plus an exact binary cofactor, with explicit zero/sign, factor-certificate provenance, basis escape, overflow, and validity state. Native operations may include compose, checked cancellation, valuation, divisibility, partial `gcd`/`lcm`, and basis projection. Factorization, reconstruction, numeric comparison, ordinary addition, and normalization must remain visible crossing operations, never implicit adapters.

This review found no strong peer-reviewed evidence for a general-purpose processor whose canonical representation of every arbitrary exact integer is its complete prime factorization. That is a **bounded literature-search result only**. It is not a claim of absence, priority, or patent freedom. Build 000's shared-substrate simulator, cost model, instruction discipline, and workload receipts should be described as bounded engineering contributions at that level.

## 9. Limitations

- The historical pass is focused and not globally complete. Chinese rod numerals, Indian decimal place value, quipu, Islamic transmission, African counting systems, analogue machines, ternary architectures, Colossus, Soviet and Japanese developments, and later reversible machines would require dedicated treatment if a later build depends on them.
- Incised prehistoric artifacts such as the Ishango and Lebombo bones were not used as secure evidence of arithmetic. Incisions are physical facts; interpreting them as numerical tallies is contested and unnecessary to this build's conclusion.
- Museum and university pages are authoritative for artifacts and archives but compress historiographic disputes. Their priority claims are not independent adjudications.
- The review did not perform an exhaustive database, dissertation, non-English-language, commercial-product, or patent search. It cannot establish novelty or nonexistence.
- Exact factor coordinates model the positive-integer multiplicative monoid cleanly. Zero, sign, rational mode, basis escape, exponent overflow, and incomplete certificates require explicit representation contracts.
- Compact-representation complexity must not be confused with expanded-value complexity. Exact reconstruction is polynomial in output length while that output may be exponentially longer than a succinct description.
- This review did not establish a theorem equating factorized addition with arbitrary integer factorization. It establishes a lack of a coordinate-local addition law, valuation bounds, and the practical need to discover or retain new output factors.
- Historical evidence can show that an architecture was contingent or that alternatives existed. It cannot by itself establish present-day performance, hardware plausibility, or a universal computational foundation.
